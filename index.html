Every signal.
A clearer decision.

Designing an AI-assisted industrial IoT experience for a global technology consultancy: turning complex device telemetry into understandable, accountable decisions across desktop and mobile.

ProductIndustrial IoT intelligence platform
My roleCreative direction, information hierarchy, interface design
ChannelsDesktop web and iOS
ScopeDesign proposal · 70 screens

AI Command Center. Interface values are illustrative scenario data.

24Core desktop screensOperational journeys
24Corresponding iOS screensCore mobile adaptations
11Additional web screensProperties and dashboard widgets
11Mobile adaptationsSupporting configuration states
01

The problem

Information was available. Decision-making was the challenge.

Industrial IoT platforms connect devices, gateways and operational systems, but connectivity alone does not create clarity. Teams still need to understand which signals matter, what might be causing a problem, and which action is safe to take.

An operator may face a rising temperature, an offline device, a delayed gateway connection and a maintenance recommendation all at once. Those signals need context before they can become useful decisions.

Seeing the fleet without losing the individual asset

Support both “How is the operation performing?” and “What is happening to this particular asset?”

Prioritising attention

A firmware update shouldn't compete equally with a gateway outage that removes visibility of a whole site. Make urgency understandable rather than showing more alerts.

Making configuration understandable and controlled

Identity, connectivity, protocols, certificates, monitoring and notification policies, broken into manageable steps with clear consequences.

Introducing AI without overstating certainty

An estimate is not an observed fact, and a possible cause is not a confirmed diagnosis.

Who it serves

Operations managersPrioritise issues and coordinate responses
Field engineersInspect assets and validate physical conditions
Device administratorsConfigure equipment and control changes
Technical supportInvestigate gateway and connectivity problems
Enterprise stakeholdersUnderstand operational health at a glance
The reframe

How might we make industrial operations easier to understand and act on across desktop and mobile, while keeping evidence, permissions and human approval visible?

02

My role in the creative direction

An operational idea. A shared creative direction.

My role was to connect the product's operational complexity with a clear creative direction. I shaped the experience around one progression: understand the current state, inspect the evidence, evaluate the recommendation, and confirm the next action.

That direction extended beyond the dashboard into onboarding, alert investigation, conversational analytics, maintenance planning and administration, giving every workflow a shared visual and behavioural language.

I defined the information hierarchy, developed the interface direction and translated the experience across desktop and mobile. I also designed the supporting states (configuration forms, empty dashboards, property creation and widget setup) so the concept would hold up beyond its showcase screens.

Guiding principle Human decisions, amplified. Never replaced.
03

The concept

From monitoring tools to a connected decision-support experience.

Instead of leading only with numbers, the command center opens with an Intelligence Brief: a short explanation of the current situation, the priorities that deserve attention, and the evidence behind them. Fleet metrics, trends, activity and asset status support that summary. AI sits inside the workflows rather than in a separate chat. It can suggest a configuration, summarise related alerts, explain a forecast or draft an inspection, while the decision stays with an authorised person.

1. UnderstandWhat needs attention?
2. InspectWhich readings support that?
3. InvestigateWhat could explain the issue?
4. ActWhat is the right next step?
5. ConfirmWho approves it, and what will change?
04

Visual direction

Calm structure for complex information

Deep navy type and contextual navigation over light surfaces, white cards and restrained mint. Hierarchy comes from grouping, spacing and type, not decoration.

A recognisable intelligence layer

Pale mint panels mark every AI-assisted summary, with the same structure each time: interpretation, evidence, confidence or limits, and a next step.

Craft through consistency

Metric cards, field labels, action placement, asset and gateway metadata, and explicit language for pending, confirmed, draft and restricted states.

Deep navyContext and hierarchy
Light surfaceOperational workspace
Pale mintAI-assisted interpretation
Mint accentSelective emphasis
Visual clarity also means semantic clarity
Observed ≠ estimated Suggested ≠ applied Approved ≠ dispatched
05

Clarity in the moments where decisions happen

Access and onboarding

Guided device onboarding

Sign in, then Identity → Connect → Review. AI can propose a template, but settings stay unapplied until someone confirms them. Completion says what was saved and who confirmed it.

Rationale: simplify the sequence without hiding technical responsibility.

Alert investigation

Evidence-led investigation

Chiller Unit 7 is at 81.9°C against an 85°C safety threshold: approaching the limit, not a confirmed breach. A trend, an evidence timeline, related gateway context and an engineer checklist frame a possible cooling issue as a hypothesis that needs physical validation.

Rationale: investigate faster without confusing correlation with causation.

AI assistance

Contextual copilot

The copilot keeps device information visible while you review its recommendation, and answers come with sources and charts. A proposed inspection doesn't change the safety threshold, and approval creates a draft rather than dispatching work.

Rationale: make recommendations understandable at the moment of decision.

Everyday product work

Practical configuration and reporting

Property creation, graphical and tabular widgets, expanded charts, date-range controls and reporting actions show how the direction handles ordinary product work, not just the AI moments.

06

Across desktop and mobile

One language, different compositions.

Desktop: compare and investigate

Width lets trends, evidence, summaries and review controls sit side by side, with persistent navigation for deeper analysis and configuration.

Mobile: read in sequence, focus on tasks

Content becomes a vertical reading flow, metrics a compact grid, and desktop overlays become dedicated mobile forms.

Terminology, asset context, intelligence summaries and approval language stay the same. The layout changes to fit the surface, and the decision model stays recognisable. Mobile is an adaptation, not a smaller desktop.

07

Outcome

A 70-screen design proposal across desktop and mobile.

Demonstrated

A consistent visual direction
Connected monitoring → investigation → review paths
AI recommendations with evidence and limits
Explicit human approval boundaries
Overview screens and detailed forms on both channels

Future validation could measure

Time to identify the top-priority issue
Onboarding completion and configuration errors
Understanding AI estimates vs observed facts
Understanding draft, approval and dispatch
Confidence in choosing a safe next action

Fleet health, uptime, device counts and risk estimates in the screens are illustrative scenario data, not results.

Reflection

The strongest idea isn't the addition of AI. It's the relationship between clarity, evidence and control. A useful industrial interface makes complexity easier to navigate without making decisions seem less consequential.

“The ambition was to make every signal lead to a clearer decision, not an unquestioned one.”

A little guidance.
A world of possibilities.

Digital Wonder: making expert-led learning feel approachable, from discovering a skill to choosing a mentor.

PlatformiOS mobile app
My roleUI/UX design, visual direction, identity refresh, AI concept
Design focusDiscovery, queries, profiles and AI
ScopeConcept redesign · 9 screen designs
01

The problem

Good learning starts with the right connection.

Digital Wonder brings learners and subject experts together around practical interests such as music, cooking, craft, yoga and dance. The vision covers discovering mentors, reviewing their expertise, discussing goals and arranging sessions.

The learner's question “Who can help me learn this, and what should I know before I reach out?”

The brief named barriers around mentor discovery, information quality, communication, pricing and language. The product also had to support two ways in, browsing for inspiration and describing a specific need, without making people learn two unrelated interfaces.

Finding relevant mentorsRecognisable categories and matching imagery, with search as a direct route.
Evaluating expertiseExperience, endorsements, reviews, skills and languages grouped so learners can judge fit before messaging.
Understanding the next stepA clear goal-and-budget structure, with responses and prices easy to scan.
Design objective

Help a learner move from “I want to learn” to a relevant mentor conversation, with fewer unanswered questions.

02

My approach

A focused, brief-led redesign. I started from the project description and the eight existing screens, using their functions and sample data as the base.

Step 1

Review the existing UI

Screen purposes, repeated content, navigation variants, hierarchy, and mismatches between mentor roles and photos.

Step 2

Establish a shared language

Deep teal, warm neutrals, stronger headings, rounded surfaces and consistent primary actions.

Step 3

Refine relevance

Broader Home categories, and imagery that matches each mentor's real subject.

03

The concept

Two paths. One learning goal.

Learners can browse interests or post a specific learning need, then review responses and evaluate a mentor. Wonder AI, a new assistant, offers optional guidance along the way.

1Discover interests
2Describe a goal
3Review responses
4Evaluate a mentor
5Start a conversation
04

Visual direction

Calm foundations. Human context.

Deep green anchors primary actions, key details and Wonder AI. Warm neutrals and white surfaces keep content calm, with soft sage for assistant prompts and selected states. A bold geometric DW monogram refreshes the app's identity.

Deep tealPrimary actions
Warm neutralPage background
Soft sageSelected states
WhiteCards and fields

Typography and hierarchy

One sans-serif family keeps the UI consistent. Larger headings set context; quieter labels separate expertise, status and session pricing.

Imagery and representation

Category scenes help people recognise an interest before reading a label, and mentor photos now match their specialty.

05

Brought to life, screen by screen

Discover and define

Room for curiosity and specific intent

Home offers Music, Cooking, Craft, Yoga, Dance and more, with search for people who already know what they want. The learning-need form asks for a goal and a budget, so mentors have useful context before they respond.

Organise

Every request easy to revisit

Open and Closed tabs sit above the list. Each card groups the goal, subject path, status and response count, in one consistent card language.

Compare

The goal stays visible

“I want to learn Guitar” stays pinned at the top while learners compare mentors by name, specialty, experience and price, with sort, filter and favourites.

Build confidence

The mentor's context in one view

The header groups identity, specialty, recent activity, reviews and sessions, with the price per session prominent and Message and Favourite right below. About, expertise, endorsements, skills and languages sit under the profile tabs.

Wonder AI

A little help, right where learning begins

A floating launcher opens a compact panel over Home. “Find a mentor” and “Plan my learning” give clear starting points. The panel says plainly that AI can make mistakes, building trust before capability.

06

Outcome

A visual foundation for the next iteration.

Delivered

Eight core screens and a Wonder AI concept in one visual language: the refreshed DW logo, a green assistant, category imagery, and clearer query, profile and pricing layouts.

Intended impact

Better discovery, mentor evaluation and request organisation, with AI guidance that doesn't take people out of context. These are design intentions, not measured results.

Next steps

Validate the journeyTest browse-first, request-first and AI-assisted routes with learners and mentors.
Resolve production dataConsistent currencies and session lengths; real mentor identities and content.
Complete the product statesMessaging, scheduling, booking, and AI privacy, accuracy and error states.

Confidence comes from relevance and clear information. Digital Wonder connects discovery, learning goals, mentor context and AI guidance into one story.

Rethinking the LinkedIn dashboard: from navigation to insight

TypeSelf-initiated concept redesign
My roleSole designer: research, UX strategy, visual direction, UI
ChannelsWeb dashboard, tablet, mobile
MethodsDesign thinking, personas, journey map, wireframes, style guide
01

The problem

While using LinkedIn, I kept running into the same friction. Every essential feature is there, but it's spread across separate sections, with no central place that brings it together.

Tracking profile performance, exploring jobs or managing connections all mean moving between tabs and losing context along the way. The fragmented structure adds cognitive load and turns simple tasks into slow ones.

The issue wasn't a lack of features. It was how those features were surfaced. People need:

Quick visibility into what matters
Actionable insights instead of raw data
A sense of control over their professional journey
The reframe

How might we design a centralised experience that reduces navigation effort while making key professional insights instantly accessible and actionable?

02

Who it's for

Two personas framed the work: one person looking for a job, and one looking for people to hire. Both had the same complaint from opposite sides.

Rex Martin

30 · Software Developer · New York

Job-seeking and building his digital presence to attract recruiters.

“I want to find jobs, reviews and company updates all in one place.”

Emily James

27 · HR Recruiter · London

Gathers information on candidates and posts roles for her company.

“I want an easy, customisable dashboard where I can see events, courses and job viewers on one page.”

Customer journey map. Following the path from log-in to job application showed where context was being lost: at every hand-off between sections.

03

The concept

Dashboard-first. Instead of making people go and find information, the system brings it to them.

A modular, customisable insight dashboard that works as a personal control centre. Each element is a card-based module, so it's flexible, scalable and personal.

A personalised dashboard for every user
Profile performance: views, reach, activity
Job recommendations tailored to the profile
Network insights and suggested connections
Learning resources and events
Everything one click away
04

Setting the direction

I owned every design call. Four decisions shaped the product, and each one was a deliberate trade-off.

Card-based layout

People can prioritise and scan quickly, and future customisation won't need a redesign of the whole interface.

One-page experience

Bringing features into one view cuts deep navigation and repeated context switching.

Actionable insights first

Rather than just showing data, the dashboard highlights the next step: apply for a job, connect with a recruiter, engage with content.

Progressive disclosure

Detail appears only when it's needed, so the interface stays clean and focused.

Balancing complexity

More information vs. clarity Personalisation vs. system complexity Rich content vs. performance
05

Wireframes

Before any visual design, I mapped the new features onto a wireframe: a personalised dashboard for every user, improved left navigation with a search bar, a summary of all activities and updates, and everything one click away. A sliding-bar micro-interaction adds a playful, game-like reason to engage.

The wireframes lock in the layout before colour and imagery: a fixed left menu on desktop, a modular grid of cards (profile stats, matching jobs, people you may know, courses, job status, groups and events), and a persistent AI Assistant entry point. On mobile, the same cards stack into one scrolling column with a bottom tab bar.

Low-fidelity wireframes: structure and hierarchy first, with greyscale placeholders for imagery and logos.

06

Brought to life

The card system made the dashboard portable. The same modules (profile views, post views, search appearances, jobs matching your profile, groups, events, courses) reflow from desktop to tablet to phone without losing their hierarchy.

07

Outcome

Impact figures are projected for this concept, not measured.

↓ 30–40% Navigation time: key actions live in one place instead of across tabs.
↑ 20–30% Faster access to opportunities, with jobs and insights surfaced up front.
↑ 25–35% Feature engagement, as jobs, recruiters and learning become visible.

Qualitatively, a structured layout lowers cognitive load, and actionable insights in place of scattered data give people more confidence and control.

Moving from a navigation-heavy structure to an insight-driven dashboard, the redesign reimagines LinkedIn as a platform that doesn't just connect people. It actively guides them through their professional journey.

The final dashboard: the same structure brought to life with a calm blue palette, real job and company imagery, progress rings for profile insights and an always-available AI Assistant.

Instagram Web, rebuilt for creators

TypeSelf-initiated concept redesign
My roleSole designer: research, UX strategy, visual direction, UI
ChannelsDesktop web, light and dark themes, consistent with mobile
MethodsDesign thinking, personas, journey map, style guide
01

The problem

Instagram's mobile experience is seamless and engaging. Its web version falls short on usability, flexibility and support for creators.

I saw an opportunity to make Instagram Web a more capable, user-friendly platform for creators, professionals and everyday users. Mapping the journey surfaced four pain points.

Poor navigation
No content-management tools
No personalisation
Limited interaction features
The goal

Design a modern, intuitive, creator-friendly Instagram web experience.

Improves usability Brings consistency across devices Enhances engagement Supports content-creation workflows
02

Who it's for

Michael Sam

29 · Artist · Berlin

A professional artist who wants to showcase and sell his work from a desktop, with high-quality uploads.

“I want to upload artwork in high resolution, find potential clients and promote my latest work.”

Stephane James

34 · Influencer · London

A mother and influencer building a community of a million mothers, and planning to launch digital products.

“I want to upload longer videos, increase my reach and plan my content a month ahead.”

03

The concept

Designing for web is not copying mobile.

The idea was to keep the mobile app's familiar, sleek interface so it feels like one product, while using the desktop's space for the work creators actually do there: uploading, discovering collaborators and planning content. I benchmarked against best practices from other leading social platforms.

Light and dark modeOne toggle, top right. Better accessibility and comfort.
Simplified navigationA cleaner layout and faster access to key features.
High-resolution uploadsDesigned for creators who work on desktop.
Enhanced discoveryConnect with users, brands and collaborators.
Activity highlightsSummaries of recent activity and updates.
Content planningConceptual: room to grow into scheduling tools.
04

Setting the direction

I kept the visual system deliberately small, so the content (people's photos and videos) stays the hero. Helvetica Neue in three weights carries the hierarchy. Instagram's signature gradient is used once, as the single accent for selected states. A neutral grey scale does the rest and makes the light and dark themes a clean swap.

05

Wireframe

The wireframe sets the desktop structure before any colour or imagery: a top navigation bar with create and profile actions, a stories row, a wide feed where posts show their caption and comments side by side, a “Suggested for you” column, and an AI Assistant panel for content ideas and captions. The light and dark mode toggle sits at the top right.

Wireframe · Desktop web

06

Brought to life

Stories along the top, a wide feed, “Suggested for you” in its own column, and creator actions always within reach. It's the same product people know from their phones, scaled up for real work.

07

Outcome

A creator-friendly platform, a more engaging desktop experience and a consistent cross-platform product.

The final design keeps everything people know from the app (stories, the feed, likes and comments) and adds what desktop creators need: room to read comments next to a post, suggestions in their own column, a create button that's always in reach, and an AI Assistant for captions and hashtags.

What I took away

Designing for web is not copying mobile.
Flexibility is critical for creators.
Small features, like dark mode, have a big UX impact.
Structure plus simplicity means better engagement.

Final design · Desktop web

Connected Enterprise: one visual language, from pitch deck to a 15-ft wall

CompanyGlobal technology consultancy: Industry & Automation
My roleDesigner: visual direction, layout, illustration and production
ChannelsRFP deck, brochures, wall posters (12 ft and 15 ft), event banner
ToolsPowerPoint, InDesign, Illustrator
01

The problem

The company's Industry & Automation story covers Industry 4.0, business resilience and digitalisation. It's complex and technical, and it needed to win work in very different settings.

The same message had to persuade a client in an RFP, inform buyers in a brochure, explain a roadmap on a wall, and stop people at a busy event booth. Each format has a different audience, reading distance and attention span. Without one shared language, the brand risked looking like a different company at every touchpoint.

Read up closeProposal decks and brochures
Read across a room12 ft and 15 ft wall posters
Read in passingEvent booth banner
02

The concept

Make “connected” something you can see.

The anchor is a single framework graphic: a pentagon of interlocking pieces (Connected Worker, Smarter Factory, Connected Product, Connected Customer, Smarter Service) around a core of data and intelligence. Every piece of communication works as a zoom in on one part of that whole.

03

Setting the direction

I defined a compact visual system that could stretch from a 16:9 slide to a 15-ft print without being redrawn.

Night ground, mint signal

Deep navy and teal covers for impact. Light interiors for reading. Mint marks what connects.

The hexagon module

A repeatable cell for icons, photos and services, which tiles into covers, slides and diagrams.

People in the system

Photography of real workers on real factory floors keeps the technology human.

Linework as connection

Fine network lines and circular crops tie slides, spreads and posters into one family.

04

How it came to life across channels

RFP proposal deck

PowerPoint · 16:9

“Technology partner for Intelligent Enterprise.” I shaped the proposal's story and design around the client's needs: who we are, our experience, client stories and how we can engage.

Result: the proposal was approved, and the client was highly satisfied.

Brochures

InDesign · Print

Two brochures, Connected Worker and Industry and Automation, each a close-up on one piece of the framework.

Dark covers carry the hexagon and photography. Inside, clean layouts and clear type guide the reader through services and success stories, so dense information stays accessible.

Wall posters

Illustrator · 12 ft and 15 ft

At wall scale the system changes job: from persuading to explaining. Light grounds, colour-coded swimlanes and the same rounded modules turn programme plans and phases into something a team can read across the room.

Event wall banner

Large format · [Event name, year]

The framework graphic, scaled up to be the booth's centrepiece: the one image that sums up the whole offer for people walking past.

Result: the banner contributed to the success of a major event.

05

Outcome

One idea, one system, many formats. The RFP was approved, the client was highly satisfied, and the event banner helped make a major event a success.
